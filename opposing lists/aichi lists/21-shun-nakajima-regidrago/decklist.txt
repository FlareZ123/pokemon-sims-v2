Usage:
This is Shun Nakajima's 21st-place CL Aichi 2026 Regidrago VSTAR list. It is a balanced toolbox with both Budew and Wobbuffet leads, Hisuian Goodra VSTAR for defensive conversion, two Crispin for basic-Energy burst, and the usual Timeless/Trifrost/Dragapult/Noivern/Koraidon attack package. The brain should treat lead ordering and the transition from lead to attacker as part of the setup graph.

The setup shell is dense: four Mysterious Treasure, four Quick Ball, three VS Seeker, Dedenne-GX, Crobat V, Squawkabilly ex and Tapu Lele-GX provide access and draw, while Battle Compressor/discard costs place the correct Dragon payloads. Three Double Dragon Energy plus two Crispin and Raihan create multiple Energy routes. Because only three Regidrago V are present, preserving a second attacker is important in mirrors and against gust-heavy control.

Budew denies Items with a zero-Energy attack and is strongest when the opponent's first decisive route is Item-dependent. Wobbuffet is a different lead: while Active, Bide Barricade suppresses non-Psychic Pokémon Abilities in play, hands and discard. It can shut down Iron Thorns ex's Initialization, Garbotoxin, Dedenne/Crobat-style engines and other non-Psychic setup nodes, but it also suppresses this deck's own non-Psychic support Abilities while Active. Tapu Lele-GX is Psychic and remains an important exception. The brain must model which own connectors reopen or close under Wobbuffet rather than treating it as free disruption.

Lead order matters when both leads are useful. Wobbuffet first can deny an opposing Ability-driven setup turn; Budew afterward can deny the Item-based escape/rebuild turn, or vice versa if Items are the immediate threat. They cannot both occupy the Active at once, and every pivot consumes a switching/retreat resource. Latias ex, Guzma and ordinary retreat routes should therefore be part of the lead valuation.

Hisuian Goodra VSTAR's Rolling Iron is the defensive mirror/control payload. Dowsing Machine's Aichi analysis notes that Goodra became stronger as many lists cut Pokémon Ranger, because its damage reduction can break the usual Timeless-GX into follow-up knockout sequence. This exact list also uses Noivern ex, Kyurem, Dragapult ex, Koraidon ex and Dialga-GX, so attack selection should be based on the opponent's surviving routes rather than highest damage.

Counter Catcher becomes available while behind in Prizes and can pair naturally with a sacrificed Budew/Wobbuffet lead. Guzma remains the general gust/switch Supporter. Path to the Peak should be sequenced after needed Rule Box Abilities; Parallel City manipulates Bench size and liabilities. Megaton Blower is held for the Tool/Stadium state that otherwise blocks the winning attack or lock escape.

Primary objective: use the correct lead to disconnect the opponent's fastest ALS, transition cleanly into an attack-capable Regidrago, then choose between Timeless extra-turn conversion, Rolling Iron durability, spread, control and gust according to which line produces the first decisive state.
